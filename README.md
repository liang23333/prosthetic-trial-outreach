# Prosthetic Trial Outreach Project: Clinical Trial Gaps, Site Accessibility & Community Outreach Strategy

A comprehensive assessment evaluating current lower limb prosthetic clinical trials, participant diversity gaps, site accessibility around **Shirley Ryan AbilityLab** (Chicago, IL), and strategic outreach solutions for underserved prosthetic users.

---

## Executive Summary

Lower extremity amputation affects millions globally, disproportionately driven by dysvascular disease (diabetes and peripheral artery disease) and impacting underserved, racial minority, and elderly populations at significantly higher rates. Despite this reality, modern lower-limb prosthetic research remains skewed towards younger, male, traumatic/military amputees evaluating high-mobility (K3/K4) devices.

This project delivers:
1. **Clinical Trial Landscape & Gap Analysis**: Quantitative evaluation of 340+ lower-limb prosthetic studies on ClinicalTrials.gov and deep-dive analysis of 10 contemporary trials.
2. **Community Outreach Site Assessment**: Comprehensive geospatial and accessibility audit of **Shirley Ryan AbilityLab** (355 E Erie St, Chicago) within a 500-meter radius, evaluating healthcare infrastructure, pharmacies, public transit, and accessible parking.
3. **Actionable Community Outreach Strategies**: Five-pillar strategic framework designed to bridge the demographic gap in prosthetic clinical trial recruitment.

### 3. Systematic Recruitment Gaps & Underserved Groups

Our cross-trial analysis reveals six acute systemic disparities:

1. **The Dysvascular & Diabetic Paradox**:
   - *Clinical Reality*: Over **82% of all lower limb amputations in the United States** stem from vascular disease, diabetes mellitus, and peripheral arterial disease (PAD). Black and Hispanic Americans experience dysvascular amputation rates 2 to 4 times higher than non-Hispanic White Americans.
   - *Trial Reality*: >85% of advanced bionic and robotic prosthetic trials explicitly exclude diabetic and dysvascular patients due to healing risks, neuropathy, or sensorimotor variability. Consequently, emerging innovations are validated almost exclusively on younger, traumatic amputees.
2. **Mobility Stratification Bias (K-Level Discrimination)**:
   - Most advanced foot, knee, and neural interface trials restrict eligibility to **Medicare Functional Classification Level (MFCL) K3 and K4** (active community ambulators and athletes).
   - The majority of community amputees function at **K1 or K2** (household or limited community ambulators). Excluding K1/K2 users prevents lower-mobility individuals from accessing energy-efficient and fall-prevention technologies.
3. **Severe Geriatric Underrepresentation**:
   - The average age of lower extremity amputees in the US is >62 years.
   - However, numerous trials enforce strict upper age caps (e.g., ages 18–65 in NCT06391697, NCT06636136, NCT06737770), artificially isolating elderly individuals who face the highest risk of post-amputation mortality and isolation.
4. **Gender Disparities**:
   - Due to the high proportion of trials funded or hosted by the VA/DoD (over 10% of all trials) and trauma etiologies, male participants frequently comprise **75% to 85%** of study populations. Women amputees encounter unique socket fitting, pelvic biomechanics, and aesthetic challenges that remain under-researched.
5. **Language & Socioeconomic Exclusions**:
   - Several US trials enforce English-only reading and writing comprehension (e.g., NCT06214026). Few protocols provide Spanish or multilingual consent forms or patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs).
   - In-person protocol burdens (e.g., 5 to 10 laboratory visits with 3D motion capture) create prohibitive barriers for working-class and transit-dependent participants.
6. **Bariatric & Weight Exclusions**:
   - Rigid limits capping weight at 250–275 lbs (113–125 kg) or BMI at 35–40 kg/m² are universal across mechanical testing protocols, excluding a substantial subset of patients with metabolic syndromes.

## Part 3: Strategic Outreach Framework for Underserved Prosthetic Users

To overcome systemic trial enrollment biases, we propose a five-pillar outreach program centered at Shirley Ryan AbilityLab:

### Pillar 1: Targeted Community & FQHC Referral Pipelines
* **South & West Side Chicago FQHC Alliances**: Establish formal partnerships with Federally Qualified Health Centers (e.g., Near North Health Service Corp, Erie Family Health Centers, Friend Health) and Cook County Health (John H. Stroger, Jr. Hospital).
* **Diabetic Foot & Wound Care Clinics**: Embed trial navigators in high-volume wound clinics and vascular surgery centers to identify pre- and post-amputation patients prior to secondary loss of mobility.
* **VA Jesse Brown & Hines VA Linkage**: Coordinate with local VA amputation system of care clinics to enroll diverse veteran and non-veteran candidates.

### Pillar 2: Inclusive Protocol & Eligibility Redesign
* **Expanding K-Level Inclusivity**: Advocate for sub-studies dedicated specifically to **K1 and K2 ambulators**, testing safety, fall prevention, and stability rather than high-impact athletic performance.
* **Elimination of Arbitrary Age & Weight Cutoffs**: Transition from chronological age caps (e.g., 65 or 70) to physiologic functional readiness assessments; utilize heavy-duty dynamometers and bariatric-rated testing components up to 350 lbs.
* **Diabetic Cohort Stratification**: Instead of categorical exclusion, institute controlled diabetic sub-cohorts with active glycemic and dermatological monitoring.

### Pillar 3: Transit Subsidies & Zero-Barrier Participant Logistics
* **Comprehensive Travel Support**: Provide prepaid ADA-accessible rideshare credits (Uber WAV / Lyft Assisted), CTA pass vouchers, or 100% SRAL parking validation.
* **Accessible Lodging Partnerships**: Negotiate subsidized accessible room blocks with adjacent Streeterville hotels (e.g., DoubleTree, Hyatt Centric) for regional participants traveling >50 miles.
* **Mobile & Decentralized Testing**: Leverage SRAL's outpatient suburban DayRehab centers (e.g., Northbrook, Homewood, Burr Ridge) to bring sensor calibration and follow-up surveys closer to patients' homes.

### Pillar 4: Multilingual & Culturally Competent Engagement
* **Bilingual Clinical Navigators**: Hire full-time Spanish/English clinical research coordinators and recruit Polish/Mandarin on-call translation staff.
* **Culturally Tailored Educational Materials**: Produce Plain-Language and visually rich recruitment brochures, video consent aids, and translated PROMs (PLUS-M, PEQ, OPUS).
* **Trusted Messenger Campaigns**: Partner with local amputee support networks, community church health ministries, and the Amputee Coalition to host informational lunch-and-learns at SRAL.

### Pillar 5: Compensation & Participant Empowerment
* **Equitable Time Compensation**: Compensate participants ($75–$125 per visit plus meal allowance) recognizing the significant physical and logistical effort required for prosthetic research visits.
* **Participant Advisory Council**: Establish an Amputee Community Advisory Board at SRAL to review protocol designs, consent language, and recruitment strategies before trial launch.
